# Best White-Label AI Agent Platform in 2027

*Written November 2026. No affiliate links, no "starting at" pricing. If we get something wrong, tell us — the corrections are the point.*

A white-label AI agent platform is software that lets an agency, a gym chain's supplier, a college consultant, or a franchise operator sell AI agents **under their own brand** — their logo, their domain, their invoice — while someone else's infrastructure does the work. In 2027 this is the fastest-growing corner of the AI market, because every marketing agency that watched its clients buy chatbots wants a piece of the recurring fee without hiring engineers.

The honest version: white-label is a **margin** decision, not a technology one. You are buying the right to charge for work you did not build, and every word of the contract around that right matters more than the feature list.

## The three things sold under one name

| What it actually is | What you get | Who it suits | Monthly cost (realistic) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Reseller of a hosted platform** — vendor runs everything, you set a logo and a price | Dashboard, agent builder, channels, per-client billing | Agencies with sales and no engineers | $99–$600 platform fee + usage, plus per-workspace markup |
| **API + your own front end** — vendor gives models and messaging, you build the product | Full control, full responsibility, you own the UX and the bugs | Teams with at least one developer on payroll | $200–$1,500 in infrastructure + 60–200 build hours |
| **Self-hosted agent framework** — n8n, Dify, Langflow, or an open-source agent stack on your own VPS | Cheap seats, complete ownership, no vendor lock | Technical founders, IT shops, hosting resellers | $20–$120 in servers + real maintenance time |

Most "best white-label AI agent platform" lists quietly compare the first row only, because that is what affiliate links pay for. The second and third rows are where the business is actually won or lost.

## The five questions that decide it

1. **Whose credentials sit behind the agents?** If the WhatsApp Business numbers and the model API keys are on the vendor's account, your clients belong to the vendor, not to you. Ask: "If I leave in six months, do my clients keep working?"
2. **Can I bring my own WhatsApp number and my own model key?** BYO number and BYO key is the difference between a platform and a landlord.
3. **What is the per-conversation cost at 10× volume?** Ask for the price at 50,000 conversations a month, not the free tier. The markup you can charge does not scale up; the usage bill does.
4. **Where do the transcripts live, and can I export them?** In India, DPDP Act 2023 obligations follow the data. A vendor who cannot tell you the region and the export path is a compliance risk you are reselling.
5. **Who answers the client's support ticket at 11 p.m.?** White-label means the client calls *you*. Price that in before you sign the client, not after.

## What an agency actually keeps

Take one client paying ₹25,000 a month for a WhatsApp agent. Strip it honestly:

| Line | Monthly |
| --- | --- |
| Client pays you | ₹25,000 |
| Platform + usage | ₹6,000–₹12,000 |
| WhatsApp message fees | ₹1,500–₹4,000 |
| Your time: setup amortised + 1 hour of support | ₹3,000–₹6,000 |
| **Left over** | **₹4,000–₹12,000, or 16–48%** |

That is a real business at 10 clients and a hard slog at 3. The agencies that make white-label work do three things: they charge a **setup fee** (₹15,000–₹60,000) so the build is paid for once, they cap support in the contract, and they specialise in one vertical instead of selling "AI" to everyone.

## Where a white-label agent breaks

- **Branding is the easy 10%.** Logos take an afternoon. The 90% is onboarding, billing, transcript support and the client's own staff asking why the agent replied in English to a Hindi message.
- **The sub-account data model.** If every client's contacts sit in one flat table, one export mistake leaks one client's leads to another. Insist on workspace isolation — separate data per client — and test it before you sell.
- **Volume pricing flips on you.** Flat-fee plans feel safe until one client runs a broadcast to 40,000 numbers. Always pass usage through, with a written threshold.
- **Model deprecations.** The model you built on in 2026 will be retired. Whoever maintains the prompts must be able to re-test in a day, or every client is on a rotting foundation.
- **"AI" as the pitch.** Clients do not renew for AI. They renew for answered enquiries and booked appointments. Sell the outcome and the white-label becomes invisible — which is the point.

## When you should NOT buy a white-label platform

- **You have no distribution.** The platform is not the hard part; ten clients who trust you are. If you do not already have them, you are buying a licence to sell.
- **You cannot support it.** White-label means the 11 p.m. ticket is yours. One person on call, or do not start.
- **Your clients are in a regulated sector** — lending, insurance, medical advice — and you cannot write the compliance rules yourself. Reselling someone's compliance claim is not compliance.
- **You would be the third reseller of the same platform in your city.** Price wars start on day one. Compete on vertical knowledge, not on the same stack.
- **You think it is passive income.** It is a support business with a software margin.

## What a sane 30-day start looks like

Week 1: pick one vertical you already sell to. Build one agent, one channel, one qualifying script.
Week 2: charge one friendly client a real setup fee and run it live. Read 50 transcripts yourself.
Week 3: fix the questions the transcripts expose, then write the support cap into the contract.
Week 4: decide the platform question with real numbers — what your client paid, what usage cost, what your hours cost. Only now compare vendors.

Measure four numbers: **accounts live**, **gross margin per account**, **support hours per account**, **renewals**. Everything else is a demo.

## FAQ

**What does white-label mean exactly?** The client sees your brand, your domain and your invoice. The technology underneath is someone else's. Ask any vendor what stays yours if the relationship ends.

**Can I use my own WhatsApp Business numbers?** On a good platform, yes — the numbers sit under your Meta business account, and the platform only gets API access. If the numbers must be under the vendor's business account, you are renting your client list.

**Is reselling n8n or Dify as a white-label product realistic?** Yes for a technical team: it is the cheapest path and the most work. Budget maintenance, not just hosting.

**How much should I charge?** Setup fee ₹15,000–₹60,000 plus ₹8,000–₹30,000 a month per client for a single-channel agent, depending on volume. Anything under ₹8,000 a month usually loses money once support is real.

**Do I need my own LLM key?** Not at first. Do insist the contract lets you move to one later without rebuilding the client's agent.
