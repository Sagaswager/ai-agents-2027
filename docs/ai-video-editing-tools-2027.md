# Best AI Video Editing Tools in 2027

**Short answer:** in 2027, AI is genuinely good at the *mechanical* 70% of editing — cutting silence, captions, reframing to vertical, matching a script to b-roll, colour consistency, first-pass assembly. It is still mediocre at the 30% that decides whether a video is worth watching: pacing that carries a joke, knowing when to hold a shot, and taste. Buy for the 70%, keep a human for the 30%.

## What changed by 2027

- **Text-based editing won.** You edit the transcript, the timeline follows. This is now the default mental model, and it is why AI editing is usable by non-editors.
- **Reframing is solved.** One 16:9 edit exports clean 9:16 and 1:1 with subject tracking that rarely loses a face.
- **Voice is commodity.** Cloning, dubbing and lip-sync in 30+ languages are cheap and mostly good — the remaining risk is legal, not technical.
- **Generation is a shot, not a scene.** AI-generated b-roll is usable for 2–4 seconds as a cutaway. Anyone promising full scenes with consistent characters is selling you next year.

## The parts AI does better than you now

| Task | Human time | AI time | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cutting silences and filler | 20–40 min | instant | always use it |
| Captions + styling | 25 min | 2 min | use it, spot-check names |
| Reframe to vertical | 15 min | 1 min | use it |
| Rough assembly from a script | 90 min | 5 min | good first draft, expect to delete half |
| B-roll suggestion | 30 min | 2 min | useful, still your taste |
| Colour match across clips | 45 min | 5 min | use it, fix skin tones manually |
| Pacing, timing a joke, structure | — | not yet | yours |

## How to judge a tool in 15 minutes

1. **Give it a messy real recording**, not a clean demo file. One take, one mistake, one loud fan. Watch where it fails.
2. **Check the export, not the preview.** Preview resolution hides artefacts. Export, then look at 100%.
3. **Count your clicks to fix an AI mistake.** If correcting the AI takes longer than doing it yourself, the tool is not saving you time.
4. **Ask what happens to your footage.** Where it is stored, for how long, and whether it trains on your files. For client work, this is the whole decision.

## The workflow that actually saves hours

Record once, edit by transcript, let AI produce a rough cut and captions, then spend your saved 90 minutes on the parts only you can do: the opening three seconds, the story order, the ending. Most creators who "use AI editing" skip the last step and publish something that sounds like everyone else.

## Where this is heading

The 2027 direction is agents that take a goal — "cut this into 10 shorts that each stand alone, post the best three" — and return finished files. That is real, and it is exactly where tool-level editing and agent-level automation split: a tool waits for you, an agent finishes the job.

## FAQ

**Can AI edit a wedding film or a documentary?** It can assemble and caption. The storytelling, the emotional beat, the 40-minute cut — no. Anyone claiming otherwise has not delivered one.

**Is AI-dubbed audio good enough to publish?** For reach, yes. For a paid ad, check the numbers and the accent. Always disclose dubbing.

**Will this replace editors?** It replaces assistant-editor hours and makes one editor able to ship three projects. It does not replace taste, and taste is what clients actually buy.

**What should I pay?** Per-seat monthly for a tool. If a vendor prices by "AI credits" per export, model your real volume first — that is where the bill lives.
