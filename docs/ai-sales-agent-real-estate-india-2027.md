# AI Sales Agent for Real Estate in India 2027

*Written November 2026. No affiliate links, no "starting at" pricing. If we get something wrong, tell us — the corrections are the point.*

A real-estate lead in India goes cold in about **four hours**. Portal enquiries, Meta lead ads, hoardings, and WhatsApp forwards all land on the same phone, and the first person to reply properly usually wins the site visit. That is the entire job an AI sales agent is hired to do in 2027: reply in seconds, ask the four questions that qualify a buyer, book the visit, and chase the ones who go quiet — without pretending to be a human sales manager.

Here is what it genuinely does well, what it does badly, what it costs, and when you should not buy one.

## The six jobs, ranked by how well AI does them

| Job | How AI does it in 2027 | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First reply to a portal/ads lead | Replies in 5–30 seconds, 24×7, in Hindi/English/Hinglish | **Buy for this alone** |
| Qualifying: budget, area, possession date, loan status | Reliable if you script the four questions and store answers as fields | Good |
| Booking a site visit + reminder | Very good — calendar slot, confirmation, day-before reminder | Good |
| Follow-up on silent leads | Good if capped: 3 touches over 10 days, then stop | Good, with a stop rule |
| Answering project/price/legal questions | Only from documents you give it; otherwise it invents | Risky — restrict it |
| Negotiation, closing, site-visit conversation | Poor. Keep humans here | Do not buy for this |

The pattern is consistent: **AI is excellent at the first 60 seconds and the boring repeats, and bad at anything that carries a promise.** A price quote or a possession date delivered wrongly becomes a legal problem, not a bad lead.

## Where Indian real estate is different from global SaaS

Three things break imported tools:

1. **WhatsApp is the inbox, not email.** Buyers will not read email and often will not answer a phone call from an unknown number. A tool that qualifies leads in a web form is solving a problem you do not have.
2. **Hinglish is the default.** A buyer writes "2bhk me kya rate hai Whitefield me". A model that answers only formal English loses the lead. Test Hindi and Hinglish in the demo — with a real message you received, not a clean one written for the demo.
3. **Broker networks and channel partners.** Part of your pipeline arrives through people who expect a fast, human answer from a named person. An agent must recognise those numbers, answer with facts, and hand over — not run a qualification script on a broker.

Ignore the per-seat pricing on the website; the real number is per conversation plus WhatsApp's own message fees.

| Setup | Realistic monthly cost (INR) | Fit |

| Multi-project agent with CRM sync and site-visit booking | ₹25,000–₹60,000 + message fees | Mid-size builders, 3+ projects |
| Portal + ads + voice follow-up, human handoff, dashboards | ₹60,000–₹1,50,000 | Large builders, ₹50 cr+ inventory |

The DIY number is honest, not a sales argument for us: a workflow you built is a workflow you maintain. WhatsApp Business API template changes, number migrations, and model deprecations will each cost you an evening. Price your own evening before you decide DIY is cheap.

## The five questions to ask in a demo

1. **Send a real Hinglish message from my own phone — what does it reply?** Not a scripted demo. Your message.
2. **Show me where the budget and area answers are stored.** If the answer is "in the chat", you have a chat toy, not a sales agent.
3. **What happens when the buyer asks a question the agent does not know?** The only acceptable answer is: it says it will check and hands to a human, with the transcript.
4. **Who owns the number and the transcripts if I stop paying?** Get this in writing. Exit cost is the most common hidden cost.
5. **After a site visit is booked, what stops it from messaging the buyer again?** A stop rule that works, or you will burn your best leads on reminders.

## When you should NOT buy one

- **You have fewer than 30 leads a month.** A human with a good WhatsApp Business quick-reply set is cheaper and better. This is not a close call.
- **You cannot answer the qualifying questions yourself in writing.** An agent that guesses budget ranges creates angry buyers and, occasionally, a legal notice.
- **Your inventory and pricing change weekly.** An agent reading a stale price list is worse than no agent. Fix the source document first.
- **Your sales process is one senior person's relationships.** Do not automate a relationship. Automate the queue in front of it.
- **You cannot name the human who receives the handoff.** An agent with no escalation path is a complaint generator.

## What a working rollout looks like

Week 1: one project, one channel (Meta ads or one portal), the four qualifying questions, and a hard handoff to two named salespeople.
Week 2: add site-visit booking and the day-before reminder.
Week 3: add follow-up with a stop rule — three touches, ten days, then out.
Week 4: read the transcripts yourself, 50 of them. Fix the questions, not the model.

Measure four numbers and nothing else: **first-reply time**, **qualified rate**, **site visits booked**, and **site visits attended**. If visits attended do not move, the agent is not working, however good the transcript looks.

## FAQ

**Can an AI agent call buyers?** Voice agents exist and work for confirmations and reminders. For a first call to a new lead, most Indian buyers hang up on a synthetic voice, and TRAI/DND rules apply. Use voice for existing leads, not cold ones.

**Will it work in Hindi and Hinglish?** Yes, in 2027, if you test it with your own messages. Do not accept a demo in English and assume it translates.

**Do I need the WhatsApp Business API?** Yes, for anything beyond a single phone. That means a verified business, templates approved for outbound messages, and per-message fees.

**Can it read my project brochure?** Yes, and it should — restricted to that document. An agent answering from a brochure is accurate; an agent answering from the open internet is not.

**Who should run it?** One person owns the transcript review weekly. That single habit is what separates a working agent from an ignored one.
