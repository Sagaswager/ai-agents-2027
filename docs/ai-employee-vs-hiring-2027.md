# AI Employee vs Hiring in 2027: The Honest Maths

**Short answer:** an "AI employee" in 2027 is not one thing — it is a set of narrow agents with a job description and a review step. It beats a junior hire at volume work with a written process (outbound, first-line replies, research, reporting, editing). It loses to a human at anything requiring judgement, trust, or a relationship. The right question is not "AI or human", it is "which task, and who checks it".

## Where the maths actually works

| Task | AI cost model | Beats a human? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-line inbound replies (chat, WhatsApp, email) | per conversation | Yes, on speed and coverage |
| Outbound qualification calls | per minute | Yes, on volume — not on closing |
| B2B lead research and enrichment | per record | Yes, clearly |
| Video first-pass editing | per export | Yes, on mechanical cuts |
| Weekly reporting and reconciliation | per run | Yes, if the source is clean |
| Negotiation, pricing exceptions | — | No |
| Anything with a real relationship | — | No |
| Processes nobody wrote down | — | No, it makes the mess faster |

The pattern: **volume + written process + a checkable output** is automatable this year. Everything else is a human with better tools.

## The real cost, three lines

1. **Subscription** — the headline number, usually the smallest.
2. **Review time** — the hours a human spends checking output. In month one this is often larger than the work saved. Budget it or the project dies at week three.
3. **Fixing the process** — the agent will expose every undefined rule in your business. That is a feature; it is also unpaid work.

Anyone quoting only line one is selling a demo.

## Which job to automate first

Not the biggest one. The one with: a written process, a clear output someone can grade, high volume, and low consequence if wrong. In most small companies that is **follow-up** — the list of people who asked something and never got a second message. It is unglamorous, it is measurable, and it pays for itself in a month.

Second: **first-line replies outside working hours.** Third: **research before a call.**

Automate in that order. Companies that start with "replace the sales team" get one expensive quarter and a bad taste.

## What an AI employee cannot do, at any price

- Own a relationship or a promise.
- Decide to break a rule for a good reason.
- Notice that the whole plan is wrong.
- Take legal or reputational risk for you.

Every serious deployment keeps one human accountable for each agent. That person's job becomes: read the log, grade the output, correct the rule. That is a real job, and it is where the money is saved.

## FAQ

**Will it replace my team?** No. It changes the mix: fewer hours on volume, more on judgement. Teams that shrink headcount usually do it by not backfilling, not by firing.

**Is "multi-agent" real?** Yes, as a workflow where several narrow agents hand off to each other with a shared record. It is not a single AI with a personality doing your whole company.

**How long to see a return?** One workflow, 30 days, three numbers. If those three do not move, stop and pick a different workflow.

**What is the biggest mistake?** Buying per department before proving one workflow end to end.
