# Best AI Agency for Small Business in 2027

**Short answer:** pick the agency that will tell you what *not* to automate, show you a working agent on your own data before you pay, and hand you the prompts, the numbers and the exit. In 2027 that is rarer than it sounds — most "AI agencies" are a reseller of someone else's platform with a landing page.

## The four questions that do the sorting

| Question | What a builder answers | What a reseller answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Show me an agent working on my data, not your demo." | A sandbox in days, on your FAQs | A recorded demo, then a scoping call |
| "What does this cost per conversation, on top of your fee?" | A number, in writing | "It depends" |
| "If I cancel, what do I keep?" | Prompts, data, transcripts, docs | A dashboard you lose access to |
| "Which of my processes should stay manual?" | Names two or three, honestly | "Everything can be automated" |

Question four is the whole test. An agency with no answer for "what should stay manual" has not run this before on a business like yours.

## What fair pricing looks like in India

Three shapes, and each one has a failure mode:

- **Monthly retainer** — predictable, but check what happens in a month with no work. Ask for a build fee plus a smaller run fee, not one blended number.
- **Per-conversation** — aligns incentives, punishes you for success if the cap is wrong. Ask for the cap in writing.
- **Fixed project** — cleanest for a first engagement. Scope it to one use case with three measurable numbers, not "AI transformation".

Any quote that arrives before someone has read your actual enquiries is a guess. Ask what happens if the first 30 days produce nothing.

## The contract clauses that matter more than the price

1. **Data ownership.** Your transcripts, your prompts, your fine-tunes. Written down, not implied.
2. **Portability.** Can you export conversations in a format you can re-import elsewhere?
3. **Model independence.** If they hard-wired one vendor's API, what happens when the price changes?
4. **Access.** Whose account holds the WhatsApp number and the cloud project — theirs or yours? Yours, always.
5. **Notice and handover.** Two weeks of overlap should be enough for a good agency to document what they built.

## Who should not hire one

- If your enquiry volume is small and your reply time is the only problem, fix the process first. An agency cannot make eleven leads a week into a pipeline.
- If your answers are not written down anywhere, the first paid month will be spent interviewing your own team. That is fine — just budget it as content work, not engineering.
- If you want a headcount replacement, you will buy a disappointment. Agents absorb repetitive first-response work; they do not carry a relationship.
- If nobody internal can own the agent after handover, do not start. Every successful deployment we have seen has one named person inside the business who reads transcripts weekly.

## A 30-day shape that de-risks it

Week 1: one use case, your data, working agent in a sandbox. Week 2: human reads every transcript, corrections logged. Week 3: agent handles the top questions inside limits you set. Week 4: numbers reviewed — reply time, resolution rate, follow-ups recovered. Then decide on phase two, with evidence.

## FAQ

**What should a first project cost?** Less than a hire, more than a subscription. Judge it per use case with a fixed scope, and insist the second phase is optional.

**Do I need an agency at all?** No — if you have one person who enjoys settings and a platform with good docs, you can configure it yourself. Agencies are for speed and for the parts you will never enjoy.

**How do I check the work is real?** Ask for the transcripts, not the dashboard. Transcripts show the mistakes; dashboards show the message count.

**Is "AI agency" regulated in India?** Not as a category. Data processing is — ask where data sits and who can read it, and expect a plain answer.
