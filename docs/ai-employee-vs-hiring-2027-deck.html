The honest maths · 2027

AI Employee
vs Hiring

Where an agent beats a junior hire, where it loses badly, and the order to automate in.

10 slides · reads in 4 minutesWritten from a live deployment, not a demo

The short answer

An "AI employee" is not one thing

It is a set of narrow agents, each with a job description and a review step.

It wins at

Volume work with a written process and a checkable output: first-line replies, outbound qualification, research, reporting, first-pass editing.

It loses at

Anything needing judgement, trust, or a relationship. And anything nobody wrote down — it just makes the mess faster.

The real question is not "AI or human". It is "which task, and who checks it".

Where the maths works

Where it loses

Three places no price fixes

Negotiation

Pricing exceptions, discounts, "can you do better". A human holds the line; an agent guesses.

Real relationships

Promise-making, trust, the awkward conversation that keeps an account.

Processes nobody wrote down

Automate an undefined rule and you get the mess, faster and more confidently.

The pattern: volume + written process + checkable output = automatable this year. Everything else is a human with better tools.

The real cost

Three lines, not one

1. Subscription

The headline number — usually the smallest.

2. Review time

Hours a human spends checking output. In month one this often exceeds the work saved. Budget it or the project dies at week three.

3. Fixing the process

The agent exposes every undefined rule you have. A feature — and unpaid work.

Anyone quoting only line one is selling a demo.

Which job first

Not the biggest one

Pick the task with all four: a written process, a clear output someone can grade, high volume, low consequence if wrong.

1

Follow-up

The people who asked something and never got a second message. Unglamorous, measurable, pays for itself in a month.

2

After-hours first line

Replies when nobody is at a desk. The queue that otherwise dies overnight.

3

Research before a call

Boring, high-value, easy to grade: did it find the right facts or not.

Start with "replace the sales team" and you buy one expensive quarter and a bad taste.

What no price buys

An agent cannot…

  • Own a relationship or a promise.
  • Decide to break a rule for a good reason.
  • Notice that the whole plan is wrong.
  • Take legal or reputational risk for you.

Every serious deployment therefore keeps one human accountable per agent.

That is not a limitation of the tool. It is the design.

The job that gets created

Read the log. Grade the output. Correct the rule.

That is the accountable human's job once agents are running — and it is where the money is actually saved.

Not a headcount cut

Teams that shrink usually do it by not backfilling, not by firing. The mix changes: fewer hours on volume, more on judgement.

Multi-agent, honestly

Several narrow agents handing off to each other over a shared record. Not one AI with a personality running your company.

One workflow, 30 days, three numbers. If they do not move, stop.

The biggest mistake

Buying per department

Before one workflow is proven end to end — measured, graded, corrected — every additional department is a second unfinished project.

Instead

One workflow. Thirty days. Three numbers. Then decide what is next with evidence instead of enthusiasm.
